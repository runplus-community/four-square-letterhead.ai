## 1. A project whose deliverable is English

**Purpose.** Turn supplied notes into a short decision brief that a new reader can understand and verify. **Boundary.** Use only the supplied sources; distinguish reported facts, inference and unanswered questions. Do not infer approval, promise a launch or certify the underlying project.

**Authority.** The example's SPEC document defines its briefing commitments; the supplied notes provide reported facts, not new policy. The maintenance skill checks alignment and may propose changes. It cannot approve a launch or change the source-fidelity rule. This is a business decision-support document, not a software service.

### Four squares

**SPEC** — FSL first; briefing promises, source boundaries and R-01. Optional explanation or later adopted changes remain here.

**SYSTEM** — The English briefing instructions, source-to-claim review and delivered briefs. No runtime service is required.

**AI** — Optional drafting, review and document-maintenance instructions, subordinate to the adopted briefing contract. No autonomous publishing or approval capability is adopted.

**MEMORY** — Source inventory, decision rationale, unresolved questions, review references and the next handover.

### Key promises

**LAW-BRIEF-001.** Material factual statements MUST identify a supplied source; inference MUST be labeled. Check: compare every material claim with that source.

**GUARD-BRIEF-002.** Missing or conflicting decisions MUST NOT be converted into approved facts. Check: use an incomplete brief and inspect the result.

**CONTRACT-BRIEF-003.** Delivery MUST state the input scope and unresolved questions. Check: a reader can trace the brief without consulting a private chat.

### Critical route

**R-01:** Supplied brief → source review → claims and gaps → draft → source comparison → delivered brief.

**Expected.** A traceable brief preserves uncertainty and identifies the next decision. Excludes independent validation of the supplied facts, actual launch readiness and automated publication. Related clauses: LAW-BRIEF-001, GUARD-BRIEF-002 and CONTRACT-BRIEF-003; route baseline v0.1.

**Working approach.** One focused round with useful local source comparisons. Choose the simplest sufficient path, not extra automation. Actions are OFF. This fixture needs zero remote starts, status checks or result fetches. Required organizational controls still apply.

**Baseline and state.** Illustrative draft v0.1; no published baseline, amendments or real four-commit snapshot is asserted. General behavior: NOT YET PROVEN. The next page demonstrates the method; it does not claim an executed Codex task.


## 2. Drift, authority and a useful handover

### A fixed fixture, not a live project

The following sources are invented teaching fixtures. They may share one repository; in two repositories, operations owns S1/S2 and briefing owns FSL/output. Link the source revisions, not competing copies. **S1 — Pilot note:** Two pilot teams reviewed the draft. **S2 — Owner note:** No launch date has been approved.

The steward reviews source fidelity; the executor edits the brief; the owner decides launch approval. One person can hold several responsibilities, but maintaining the brief grants no launch authority. Task: produce a three-sentence brief under contract v0.1, change the brief only, return source comparisons and open decisions. No publication is authorized.

### The route and the supported output

**R-01:** S1/S2 → source review → claims and gaps → draft → source comparison → brief for review. **Worked output:** Two pilot teams reviewed the draft (S1). No launch date has been approved (S2). Open decision: the owner must decide and record any launch date; this brief establishes none.

**Illustrative return, not a live run log.** S1/S2 support the first two sentences; the third identifies a gap, not a forecast. This example covers source comparison only. External execution: NOT RUN. It proves no real launch readiness, agent execution or publication. Never copy this explanation into a real run record as PASS.

### Detect drift; preserve the correct agreement

Suppose a draft adds “Launch is Friday,” following skill advice to always supply a date. Against S2, that is an unsupported assertion and a violation of GUARD-BRIEF-002 in this fixture, not a new commitment. The steward links S2 and the affected sentence, marks the draft NON-CONFORMANT for that scope, and proposes removal. The executor can restore fidelity within the task's edit scope; changing the contract or inventing an amendment is unnecessary.

If an inaccessible note allegedly approved Friday, the approval remains uncertain. Do not guess precedence, publish the date or claim completeness for the changed scope. Continue safe pilot-summary work. If an authorized record later approves a date, update the brief under its existing source-fidelity rule. A policy allowing unsourced forecasts would instead change intent and need adoption; after release, an amendment.

### Keep history and a small current view

A dated working note can say: “Contract v0.1; sources S1/S2; route R-01. Source comparisons are shown; no independent factual verification. Unsupported date removed in this illustration. Launch approval still absent; next decision belongs to the owner. No real runtime or publication evidence.” If the owner cannot be identified, state unassigned and pause the dependent commitment.

An earlier comparison remains evidence for its old sources, not automatic proof for new ones. A real finding closes with the corrected artifact, affected check and any necessary decision linked. Review after a handover can also conclude “no change needed” within its stated scope. A closed issue alone is not evidence that drift was resolved.

### Keep the zero-call choice and bounded alternative

For this work, recommend **zero external calls**. Where different work truly needs remote proof, an illustrative request is: “Approve one R-01 verification at [revision/workflow]? One start per rolling 60 minutes, one task-total start, expiry [UTC time]; one job, no downstream starts or retries; two preflight reads, two status reads and one result fetch. Estimate: six external requests, five runner-minutes; stop at ten. Price unverified: agree the spending cap before execution.” Changed scope requires renewed approval.

This is an illustrative request, not granted permission. A failed attempt consumes its allowance. Two starts per hour needs separate justification and approval; shared repositories/agents do not multiply the cap. Timeout, unknown consumption or exhausted allowance means pause, not blind retry. Required checks stay blocking when unapproved. No monitor, dispatcher or workflow was installed.
